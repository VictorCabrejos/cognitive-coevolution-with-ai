## Keep the reasoning after the answer closes

**Time:** about 15 minutes. **Materials:** paper or a plain document; an assistant is optional.

### Source card

Fictional source card: a club's poster says, “Bring a reusable cup. A paper cup is available if needed.” A proposed reminder says, “Everyone must bring a reusable cup or cannot attend.”

### Steps

1. First attempt (3 minutes): write a reminder that preserves both the request and the fallback. Underline the source words supporting it.
2. Check (3 minutes): identify exactly what the proposed reminder added. Explain why that addition changes the invitation.
3. Review (3 minutes): if an assistant is available, ask it for one challenge to your reminder, using only the source card. Otherwise compare your reminder with the source clause by clause. Keep or reject the challenge with a reason.
4. Transfer (4 minutes): close your reminder. New source: “Bring a notebook. Spare paper is available.” Write a faithful reminder and explain the parallel without copying.
5. Checkpoint (2 minutes): record what you changed, what you can now explain and one question still open.

### Expected deliverable

Two short reminders, source support for each, and a three-line learning note.

### Reflection checklist

- [ ] Did I preserve the fallback condition?
- [ ] Did I check the assistant or proposed reminder rather than accept its confidence?
- [ ] Could I handle the changed case with the earlier answer closed?
- [ ] Have I separated a successful attempt from a claim of lasting learning?

Keep your first attempt before consulting the [sample response and rubric](answers.md#activity-self-review). You can pause, save an open question and return later.
