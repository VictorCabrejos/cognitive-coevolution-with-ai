### 4. Sample answer

The second total is 15 + 35 + 10 = 60 minutes, a 30-minute saving against 90. The examples demonstrate the arithmetic and categories. They do not measure a real workflow or forecast later savings; repeated relevant observations would be needed.

### 5. Sample answer

Preserving attention, health, freedom and manageable maintenance can serve the person's purpose. A commitment may consume the capacity the arrangement was meant to return. The decision still depends on the actual benefit, full costs and human constraints rather than a rule that expansion or refusal is always best.

## Activity self-review

### One defensible response

First total: 70 minutes, saving 20. Second total: 60, saving 30. A useful card asks what was measured, who supplied the data, which comparison supports improvement and what remains unknown. Its use note preserves first-attempt-before-feedback order and triggers review when numbers, audience or sources change. These hypothetical totals support no promise about another person's savings.

### Five-point review

Give yourself one point for each criterion your work demonstrates. A different conclusion can earn the point when it meets the criterion and is supported by the supplied information.

1. Both totals and savings are calculated correctly.
2. The card distinguishes confidence from demonstrated skill.
3. The use note identifies audience, purpose, source needs and order.
4. A portability ambiguity is identified and repaired.
5. The next decision considers remaining effort and limits without forecasting guaranteed savings or demand.

**4–5:** identify one limit or improvement before reusing the work. **2–3:** revise one missing element and explain the change. **0–1:** return to the source card and work through one condition before expanding the task.

This is a practice aid, not a validated measure of competence, learning or health. Evaluate the reasoning rather than agreement with a philosophy or confidence in a response.
