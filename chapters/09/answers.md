## Explained answers

### 1. B

Nine of twelve respondents is 75%. Nonrespondents' views and the invited population cannot be substituted.

### 2. A

The memo reports a confidence response; it supplies neither an independent skill test nor later retention evidence.

### 3. C

The first attempt provides a record of the learner's reasoning. Mistakes can then guide feedback rather than be hidden.

### 4. Sample answer

“While using the guide, the trainee selected the expected next steps on 24 of 30 author-selected cases.” The percentage describes performance on those cases and conditions, not the guide's universal accuracy. Case selection, comparison with alternatives and unaided performance remain separate questions.

### 5. Sample answer

Compare a confidence response with an independently performed skill task, rather than add only division practice. Inspect whether the learner preserves that distinction in a changed example. A successful related task supplies limited evidence of applied understanding, not permanent learning or broad transfer.

## Activity self-review

### One defensible response

Source A supports: “Nine of twelve survey respondents, 75%, reported greater confidence.” It does not establish skill improvement, answers from all staff or a reason for immediate replacement. Source B supports a 24/30 performance statement under guide-assisted, author-selected conditions. A teaching note should target the actual error in the first attempt, while treating any proposed teaching improvement as still to be checked.

### Five-point review

Give yourself one point for each criterion your work demonstrates. A different conclusion can earn the point when it meets the criterion and is supported by the supplied information.

1. The first attempt was recorded before the review card was opened.
2. The Source A denominator and confidence measure are correct.
3. The repaired claim does not invent skill gains or adoption evidence.
4. The independent Source B statement preserves selection and guide-use conditions.
5. The teaching note targets an observed error and leaves its proposed benefit untested.

**4–5:** identify one limit or improvement before reusing the work. **2–3:** revise one missing element and explain the change. **0–1:** return to the source card and work through one condition before expanding the task.

This is a practice aid, not a validated measure of competence, learning or health. Evaluate the reasoning rather than agreement with a philosophy or confidence in a response.
