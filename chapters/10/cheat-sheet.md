## Key distinctions

**Separate assessment dimensions.** The chapter distinguishes purpose, evidence, rationale, control, uncertainty and attribution. A strength in one cannot cancel a failure in another.

**Disclosure and ownership.** Disclosure states assistance received. Ownership concerns the person's ability to justify and control the result. Both duties can matter.

**Local act and institutional hypothesis.** Judgment Abdication concerns a local surrender of required evaluation. AI-Assisted Epistemic Involution concerns a regressive trajectory; its institutional propagation is a bounded hypothesis, not a measured claim about all institutions.

**Fair demonstrations.** Explain, simplify or modify the work against visible criteria. A written demonstration can test the same capability and responsibility level as a spoken one.

## Everyday example

A fictional project report discloses assistant use but includes a nonexistent reference. Disclosure does not supply evidence for the reference. Conversely, understanding the work does not excuse an institution's required attribution.
