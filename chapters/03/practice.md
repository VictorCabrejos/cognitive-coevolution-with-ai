## Decide what the venue comparison can support

**Time:** about 15 minutes. **Materials:** paper or a plain document; an assistant is optional.

### Source card

Fictional venue card: A costs $180 plus $12 per attendee and has confirmed step-free access. B costs $300 plus $8 per attendee; its access is initially unconfirmed. Expect 35 attendees. The organizer requires confirmed step-free access. No booking or payment is authorized.

### Steps

1. Brief (3 minutes): name the result, beneficiary, constraints, acceptance criteria and stopping condition for this comparison.
2. Eligibility (3 minutes): state which venue can currently be treated as eligible and identify the missing check.
3. Compare (3 minutes): suppose B's access is confirmed. Calculate both 35-person costs and state exactly what the difference establishes.
4. Transfer (4 minutes): independently recalculate for 45 attendees. Explain why the price advantage changes in size without changing the access requirement.
5. Checkpoint (2 minutes): draft a review note separating the cost result, remaining booking terms and who can authorize a commitment.

### Expected deliverable

A five-part brief, two cost comparisons and a short pre-booking checkpoint.

### Reflection checklist

- [ ] Did a low price displace a required condition?
- [ ] Did I preserve why access matters?
- [ ] Can I explain both calculations?
- [ ] Does the checkpoint leave the commitment subject to actual authorization?

Keep your first attempt before consulting the [sample response and rubric](answers.md#activity-self-review). You can pause, save an open question and return later.
